SUM The brief | Imagine a future AI service and how it would reshape family life, as a speculative film and design artefacts.
SUM What we made | PATH, a fictional 2040 system that mandates and scores how Singaporean families care for their elders. It is told through a film and three interactive artefacts: an archive of one household, the website of the ministry behind it, and a 3D model of the system at work.
SUM My role | I built all three interactive artefacts, including the 3D worldbuilding game, and designed the PATH point-of-view interface used in the film. The concept was developed together as a team; the poster, hologram, logos and most of the film were made by teammates.
MEDIUM Figma, HTML/CSS/JavaScript, Three.js, Rhino
P PATH is a speculative design project set in a fictional Singapore of 2040, where the government uses AI to mandate elderly care in every household. A console and two drones sit in the living room, watch how a family looks after its elders, and turn every act of care into a score.
P Officially, PATH supports families. In practice it observes, measures and ranks them, and it misses most of what real care looks like. Its red-eyed drones borrow from HAL 9000 in 2001: A Space Odyssey, and its watchful state from Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four. The project asks one question: when care becomes a score, do we start caring for the score instead?
P Supervised by Dasom Choi for the AI x Society: Future of Family design platform.
H Design Brief
P We rarely meet AI as raw technology. It reaches us as the end product: a service, an app or an everyday interaction, and designers are the ones who shape that encounter, deciding how AI enters someone's life and changes it. For this design platform, teams were asked to imagine a future AI service and explore how it would transform family life: its structure, its relationships and its meaning. The outcome is a speculative provocation rather than a product, built to confront audiences with the values and trade-offs embedded in such a future.
P Alongside a two-minute film, each team produced a design artefact that shows how the service operates in its world. Ours became three: an interactive archive of the PATH household, the fictional website of the ministry that runs it, and a 3D model of the system at work.

H The PATH system
P PATH is a console and two PATH Drones. When the drones dock on either side of the console, the three pieces line up into a face: the drones become its eyes and the console's red dot-matrix display its mouth. Even at rest, PATH is looking back at the family.
P The drones float through the home, follow the family and come closer when something looks wrong. We wanted them to feel like attentive pets rather than CCTV. Their one caring feature, a HELP button that slides out when someone has fallen, is also how the system records who showed up and who didn't.
PART console | PATH console | path-part-console.png | path-part-console-m.png | https://nikhoftime.github.io/MFS-Website/ | Explore the console
P The PATH-C1 console measures 720 × 250 × 212 mm and weighs 7.6 kg, in black anodised aluminium. Its face is 352 red LEDs in a 32 × 11 grid, six far-field microphones listen across an 8 m radius, and a recessed HELP button on the side calls the family and notifies the ministry. Hover over the numbered callouts to see what each part does.
PART drone | PATH Drone | path-part-drone.png | path-part-drone-m.png | https://nikhoftime.github.io/MFS-Website/ | Explore the drone
P Each PATH-E2 drone is a 100 mm cube weighing 280 g. Its 60 mm domed lens records in 4K with infrared night vision, and a nine-blade aperture tightens when it locks onto a person. Four ducted rotors keep it below 30 dB, and at 23:00 both drones return to their 140 mm charging docks, and keep listening.
P Through a drone's eyes, the family becomes data. One morning in the Tan household, from 07:42 to 08:00, as PATH sees it.

H The hologram
IMG path-holo-room.png
P Floating 300 mm above the console is the household dashboard. It is projected as light, with no physical glass, so it cannot be covered. Every act of care becomes credits, and each family member's share is laid out for everyone in the living room to see.
P We designed it to look calm, clean and data-driven, the way these systems usually present themselves. Underneath, it turns love into a ledger, ranks a family against itself, and names whoever is falling behind. It cycles through six views over one week: scoring, the Sunday 21:00 audit, the overview, family rankings, behaviour patterns and the next step.
W Scoring | path-holo-v1.png | | Every act of care becomes credits the moment PATH sees it, and the household score updates live.
W Audit | path-holo-v2.png | | Every Sunday at 21:00, PATH audits the week. No one in the household is asked.
W Overview | path-holo-v3.png | | The week's score sets the household's access tier, and with it what the family can get: healthcare, grants, upgrades.
W Rankings | path-holo-v4.png | | Each member is ranked against their target, and whoever is behind is shown in red. Ah Ma's own rating is recorded separately and does not affect credits.
W Patterns | path-holo-v5.png | | PATH names the habits behind the numbers: a son's phone interruptions, a daughter's unverified meals, companionship worth almost nothing.
W Next step | path-holo-v6.png | | Each week ends with an order and a deadline, and the penalty if it is missed.

H The fictional MFS website
P A device needs an institution behind it. We invented the Ministry of Family & Society, with a vision, values, a law and a logo, and built its website as a fictional government portal from 2040 that visitors can actually use. It borrows the look and language of Singapore's government sites today. Nothing on it is sinister on its own: every feature is presented as help.

H Can AI measure care?
P PATH is built with an inherent flaw, on purpose. It is less a proposal than a question about AI in our society, approached through value sensitive design: designing with human values in mind from the start, and asking whose values a system serves, which ones it trades away, and who is affected without being asked. PATH promises safety, efficiency and accountability, and pays for them with privacy, autonomy, trust and dignity. Its most important stakeholder, Ah Ma, is the one person whose view does not count.
P Care is one of the most innately human things we do. It is patience, presence and attention: noticing that someone is tired before they say so, or sitting with Ah Ma for an hour without needing a reason. Much of it is invisible and given freely. An AI system can only measure what it can observe, so it counts proxies instead, and once those proxies are rewarded, families start performing them. Even the ministry's own website admits that "intention, affection, resentment and exhaustion are outside the scope of assessment".
P So can AI really measure how humans express care? If every act of care is counted, does anyone still do something that doesn't score? PATH is not a prediction. It is a way of looking at a future assembled from things Singapore already has, one reasonable convenience at a time, and asking what a family gives up in exchange.
