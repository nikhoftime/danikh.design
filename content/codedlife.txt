SUM The project | Coded Life is a QR code exhibition at Sengkang MRT station, one of 17 stops on New Everyday Life, By Design, a design trail along the North East Line from 14 November to 6 December 2026. It takes a fresh look at a graphic we scan every day and asks: can a simple scan become an experience?
SUM My role | I designed and built the team’s tool for Qrossword, a QR code written in handwritten letters. Instead of lettering every square by hand on paper, the team could write letters into individual squares with an iPad or mouse, copy them across the code and check that it still scanned.

H Qrossword
P QR codes are made to be read by cameras, not by people. To us, the black and white squares mean nothing. Qrossword challenges that by replacing the squares with letters that people can read, so the same code now speaks to both: a camera still scans it, and a person can read it too.
P On the pillar, it becomes a word search: you hunt for the 16 North East Line stations hidden among the letters. Even the three corner markers are lettered. Look closely and they spell out the name of the exhibition: COD, EDL, IFE, or “Coded Life”.
PAIR cl-qrossword.jpg cl-danikh.jpg
P Writing a whole code by hand meant hundreds of squares, and one weak letter could stop it from scanning. So I built a tool for the team. You write each letter into its square with an iPad or mouse, guided by a faint letter, and the tool keeps a live preview of the whole code. A built-in decoder checks after every stroke whether a phone could still read it, and ink weight and a grey backing help when it can’t. Letters already written can be copied across the rest of the code, so the team could test the full look before writing every square.
H Write your own
P I rebuilt that tool for this site. Type a link, write the letters into the squares, watch the code fill up and see whether your handwriting still scans.

H The digital twin
P The Interactive Materials Lab rebuilt the pillar online as a 3D digital twin at qrcoded.life, so the exhibition can be visited from anywhere. You can turn the pillar, zoom in and tap any QR code on it to open its story.
