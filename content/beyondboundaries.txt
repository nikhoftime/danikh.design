SUM The brief | Observe an everyday situation closely, question the habits around it, and propose how design could change the way people act or live.
SUM What we made | A system of urban planters that replaces the painted yellow smoking box with soft, green boundaries. Planters, seats, a sensor-integrated bin and an abstract responsive panel can be arranged for smoking or non-smoking areas.
SUM My role | I designed the panel's abstract graphic, both of its visuals and the live responsive panel artefact. I also built the final 3D models in Rhino, prepared the rendering files in Twinmotion and KeyShot, and prepared the STL files for most printed parts. I spray-painted the physical models, made some of the plants and narrated the film. The concept was developed together as a team; the ashtray bin, panel mould and poster were made by teammates.
MEDIUM Rhino, HTML/CSS/JavaScript, Physical Model
P Beyond Boundaries is a system of urban planters that shapes how people behave in shared spaces. Instead of painted lines, planters, seats, a sensor-integrated bin and a responsive panel use soft, green boundaries to define a space, and the same pieces can be arranged for smoking or non-smoking areas.
P For the designated smoking area, we challenged how the effects of smoking are usually shown. An abstract panel replaces graphic warnings, turning the smoking area into a place for reflection. The soft boundaries also change how the space reads from outside: people smoking inside are no longer boxed in and on display, but sitting in a planted space like anyone else.
P Supervised by Christian Boucharenc for the Improving Our Daily Lives platform.
H Design Brief
P Inspired by Kenya Hara's research on observation, Improving Our Daily Lives begins with careful discovery of problematic situations in everyday experience. Over 13 weeks, teams observe ordinary scenarios, question existing habits, routines and behaviours, define a design problem with clarity, and propose how design could offer new ways of acting, interacting or living.

H Design intervention
P Beyond Boundaries is an adaptable spatial system of four elements. Spatial planters define the edge of a space with greenery rather than a hard box. Modular seats give comfortable places for a brief stay. A sensor-integrated bin registers smoking activity while working as a familiar disposal point. A responsive panel translates that activity into an accumulating visual pattern. Without the bin and panel, the same planters and seats shape non-smoking spaces too.

SHOTS bb-plate-enclave.png bb-plate-nook.png bb-plate-trail.png
P The same pieces form three layouts. The Enclave is a C-shaped, semi-enclosed and communal space. The Nook is an L-shape that is compact and fits corners. The Trail is a linear, open configuration that is directional and space-efficient for long, narrow urban spaces. Colour does the zoning quietly: the seats in the Enclave are yellow because it is the designated smoking area, a softer echo of the yellow box, while red and blue seats mark the non-smoking layouts.
H Nature as a soft boundary
IMG bb-plate-plants.png
P The planters draw on Singapore's urban greenery. Aloe vera, philodendron, pothos, snake plant, Boston fern and peace lily soften the rigid demarcation and create a more considered spatial experience, so the edge of the smoking area is planted rather than painted.
H The sensor-integrated bin
MP4 bb-dispose.mp4
IMG bb-plate-bin.png
P The bin is a faceted, asymmetrical form with a smoke sensor inside that detects and measures the smoke concentration as each cigarette is put out. It turns a familiar disposal point into the input for the panel.
H Beyond graphic warnings
IMG bb-plate-packs.png
P Since 1 July 2020, every tobacco product sold in Singapore comes in a standardised dark brown pack, with graphic health warnings covering at least 75% of its surfaces. The panel grew out of these images and the guilt-tripping tone of anti-smoking campaigns. Knowing the consequences does not always lead to change, and shock is easy to tune out.
P So instead of another warning, the panel takes a metaphorical approach: abstract art that does not single anyone out, but lets smokers see their shared, accumulating impact and become a little more conscious of smoking's harmful effects.
H The responsive panel
MP4 bb-studio.mp4
P The visual is inspired by contrails. Right behind a plane, a contrail is crisp and bright; further back it breaks apart, softens and drifts, and it is still there long after the plane has gone. Each trail on the panel behaves the same way: one cigarette, one trail, released from the centre of the glass and gradually fading, a trace of something that has already passed.

P It works in three steps. Detect: the smoke sensor in the bin registers each cigarette as it is discarded. Translate: the reading becomes one new trail, stronger when there is more smoke, and is sent to a thin, flexible screen. Visualise: the panel builds up over time, with new trails layering over older, fainter ones.

H Encouraging reflection, not enforcing change
P Beyond Boundaries does not try to stop people smoking. It gives smokers a space with dignity, lets them coexist with the people around them, and makes the consequences of smoking a little harder to overlook.
